ISSUE

  • The first three bags were weighed on Ishida and on MT. The two numbers do not match.
  • Ishida side: 203.4, 200.9, 201.1 g. Mean of the first three is 201.80 g. Mean of bags 4–50 is 200.98 g. Almost all of that gap is bag 1.
  • Range is 4.0 g (199.4–203.4). Only bag 5 (199.8 g) and bag 6 (199.4 g) are under 200 g.
  • On-screen Average, S.D., MAX, MIN, and Range are all 0. That column was not calculated. The next page is recomputed from the 50 lines.
  • T.Weight 11.2 kg, T.Count 56. The 50 bags add up to only 10.052 kg. The extra 6 dumps have no bag number.

Screen CLR 10:44, machine time 11:38, photo time 11:27. No written tolerance was in this pack, so this is the spread around 200 g, not a pass/fail call.

ROOT CAUSE

SeenBag 1 is clearly heavy. The chute and the grate hold residue. Hard lumps sit on the hopper powder.

InferredIshida counts the hopper weight before discharge. Powder stuck on the cut gate or the wall is missing from the bag MT weighs. If the next bag shakes that residue loose, that bag runs heavy. Bag 1 at +3.4 g fits a startup slug letting go at once.

InferredIf bags 2 and 3 still miss MT, that looks like a fixed offset: empty-bag tare not the same, or a thin film left on every bag. That is a second factor, separate from the startup slug.

MissingThe three MT readings, the empty-bag tare, and proof both scales weighed the same bag. Without those, the gram difference cannot be stated.

Ruled outUnit 5 was calibrated at 600 g. The 200 / 400 / 600 g checks passed. This load cell is not the root cause.

Issue 2 · Cut gate

Lumps blocked the gate. Longer vibration is containment, not the fix.

Video · hard lumps on the hopper powder, about 6 seconds. The machine is stopped.

ISSUE

  • SDA lumps blocked the cut gate.
  • Vibration time was lengthened on the line to move the blocked powder.
  • This video shows hard lumps sitting on the fines. The edges stay sharp. They do not slump into the powder.
  • The gate is not moving. The jam itself is a line record, not a frame in this clip.

ROOT CAUSE

  • SeenThe lumps are in the hopper before they reach the gate. They are a grade coarser than the fines, so a small opening seats them first.
  • InferredSticky fines pack into the gap between a lump and the opening and build a bridge. Flow stops, and the gate does not close clean.
  • InferredThe original vibration time was shorter than this lot needed to break the bridge. After it was lengthened, the bridge collapsed. That is the improvement the line saw.
  • LimitLonger vibration does not reduce lumps in the raw material. Too long, and the hopper is overfed and sticky powder is smeared further, so bag-weight spread can grow. The 50 lines at 11:27 do not show whether they are before or after the change.

After the change, run another set and recompute the mean and SD from the lines. Bag 1 should not be 203.4 g again.

Issue 3 · Sticking

Powder stuck on the discharge path. That film is the bag-weight gap.

ISSUE

Two places hold powder: the chute downstream of the cut gate, and the grate further upstream. In the lump video the hopper wall is still mostly bright. The thick film is on the discharge path, not on the upper wall.

ROOT CAUSE

  • SeenThe residue is a smeared film. The upper chute is still bare and the lower half is fogged over, so powder was wiped along the flowing lower half, not wetted evenly over the whole chute.
  • InferredLumps are crushed as they pass the gate and paint sticky fines onto the metal. After the film forms, new powder sticks to the film and the layer grows.
  • SeenThe grate is caked on one side only. Powder stopped there. The whole mesh was not passing powder normally.
  • Ruled outHOBO reads 31.6% RH, inside 30–40% and on the dry side. The 11:57 wall has no droplets. Room humidity is not the cause of this film.
  • Still to checkMoisture in the raw material, and whether the chute was fully dry after cleaning. The photo cannot separate “powder stuck” from “washed and not dried.” The fine specks on the lower half look like either. Photograph the empty chute after cleaning, before the next feed.

This film is the mechanism of issue 1: part of the grams counted in the hopper never entered the bag.

Issue 4 · Raw material

The lumps are already in the hopper. Calibration passed, so the cause is the material.

Close view of the lumps. The full clip is on page 7. The machine is stopped.

ISSUE

  • The raw material contains lumps. They are cubes and flakes, sitting on the cone of fines, not sunk into it.
  • This is the state before the cut gate. The gate did not make these pieces.
  • The same day, before the test, Ishida finished two checks and both passed: Unit 5 calibrated at 600 g, and weight checks at 200 g, 400 g, and 600 g.

ROOT CAUSE

  • SeenThe lumps entered as lumps. The hopper did not break them. The grate holds stuck fines. Nothing in the video shows the mesh stopping these lumps.
  • SeenStatic weights passed at 200, 400, and 600 g. On the same scale, weights pass and real powder does not. The difference is how the powder flows, not the load cell.
  • InferredThis lot was not screened or delumped before the Ishida. Vibration time was added after the gate was already blocked. That is a downstream patch.
  • Common causeIssues 1, 2, and 3 leave from here. Lumps block the gate, broken lumps smear the wall, and residue makes Ishida and MT disagree.

Write the passed calibration into the record. It clears the instrument, and it points the next run at raw-material preparation rather than another calibration.

Next step

What is already done, and what has not closed the cause

Already done this run

  • Unit 5 calibrated at 600 g. Checks at 200 / 400 / 600 g passed. That clears the load cell.
  • After lumps blocked the cut gate, vibration time was lengthened. Powder could move again. That is containment.
  • The room was logged: 20.67°C, 31.6% RH, inside spec.
  • The 50 Ishida lines were kept. The on-screen statistics are 0. Use the lines from here on.

Close these before the next run

  1. Screen or delump the raw material before the hopper. Vibration time does not replace that.
  2. After cleaning and before feed, photograph the empty chute and hopper. Open them again after 10 bags and see whether the film returns.
  3. Record Ishida and MT on the same bag, with the empty-bag tare written down, for at least the first 10 bags. Only Ishida is on record now.
  4. After the vibration change, rerun and recompute mean and SD. Bag 1 should not be another 203.4 g startup bag.
  5. Reconcile T.Count 56 with the 50 bags. State whether the extra 6 dumps were trials, empty cycles, or rejects.
  6. Add lump condition and raw-material moisture to the batch record. Keep logging room temperature and humidity. They are not the cause of this run.
The next run has two checks only: bag 1 is no longer an outlier, and on the same bag, after empty-bag tare, the Ishida–MT gap matches the powder left on the gate.
